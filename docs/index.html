Speyser\thanks{Independent Researcher, Association Sciences \& Cooperation, France. \texttt{f.speyser@ik.me}}} \date{} \begin{document} \maketitle \begin{abstract} A cactus is strict $m$-gonal if every block is a cycle of length \(m\ge 5\). Specialising the free split-decomposition template of Bahrani and Lumbroso to a single length \(m\), we determine the generating functions of vertex-rooted and unrooted non-plane objects and analyse their dominant singularity. For odd \(m\) the critical value is explicit; for even \(m\) the same one-term substitution is blocked. The rooted coefficients are of square-root type. We prove that the radius of convergence \(\rho_m\) is strictly increasing, that \(\rho_m\to 1\), and that \[ 1-\rho_m=\lambda_m-\tfrac12\lambda_m^2+O\bigl((\log m)/m^2\bigr), \] where \(\lambda_m=\bigl(\log((m-1)/2)+1+W(e^{-1})\bigr)/(m-1)\) and \(W\) is the Lambert function. It is \emph{$m$-gonal} if every cyclic block has length \(m\), and \emph{strict} if it has no bridges. We enumerate free (non-plane) strict \(m\)-gonal cacti up to isomorphism, for fixed \(m\ge 5\). Bahrani and Lumbroso~\cite{Bahrani2018EJC,Bahrani2018ANALCO}, building on Cunningham~\cite{Cunningham1982} and Gioan--Paul~\cite{GioanPaul2012}, gave a split-decomposition grammar for cacti with an arbitrary set \(\Omega\) of admissible cycle lengths, in both the plane and the free setting. The case \(\Omega=\{m\}\) with \(m\ge 5\) is already included, and excludes bridges. That template does not solve the equations, locate the singularity, or compare growth rates in \(m\). The present paper carries out that analysis. After recalling the grammar (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}), we establish a parity dichotomy for the critical value \(\tau_m=s_m(\rho_m)\) -- explicit, \(\tau_m=(2/(m-1))^{1/(m-1)}\), when \(m\) is odd, and blocked by a secondary evaluation \(s_m(\rho_m^2)\) when \(m\) is even -- together with the square-root transfer \(s_{m,n}\sim C_m\rho_m^{-n}n^{-3/2}\) in both parities (Section~\ref{sec:sing}). We then prove that \(\rho_m\) is strictly increasing (Section~\ref{sec:mono}) and determine the large-\(m\) equivalent of \(1-\rho_m\) (Section~\ref{sec:limit}). Coefficients for \(m=5,6,7,8\) coincide with columns of the OEIS arrays A332648 and A332649; the contribution is the analysis, not the existence of the numbers. \section{Grammar} \label{sec:grammar} Let \(s_m(x)\) be the ordinary generating function of vertex-rooted strict \(m\)-gonal cacti, with \(x\) marking vertices. A cactus with \(k\) blocks has \(n=1+(m-1)k\) vertices (Figure~\ref{fig:cactus}), so \(s_m\) is supported on \(n\equiv 1\pmod{m-1}\). For \(m\ge 5\) the cycle \(C_m\) is prime~\cite[Lemma~1]{Bahrani2018ANALCO}. The reduced split-decomposition tree then has only star-nodes and prime nodes labelled \(C_m\), with the adjacency rules of~\cite[Theorem~2]{Bahrani2018ANALCO} specialised to \(\Omega=\{m\}\) (no bridges). The local constraints are summarised in Figure~\ref{fig:adj}. \begin{figure}[ht] \centering \begin{tikzpicture}[thick, font=\small, leaf/.style={circle, draw, fill=black, inner sep=1.2pt}, box/.style={draw, rounded corners=1pt, inner sep=3pt, font=\scriptsize}] \begin{scope}[local bounding box=ok1] \node[box] (S1) at (0,0.55) {star}; \node[leaf, label=below:leaf] (L1) at (0,-0.55) {}; \node[font=\scriptsize] at (0,1.05) {centre}; \draw (S1)--(L1); \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (0,-1.15) {allowed}; \end{scope} \begin{scope}[shift={(3.3,0)}, local bounding box=ok2] \node[box] (S2) at (-0.7,0.35) {star}; \node[box] (P2) at (0.7,0.35) {$C_m$}; \node[font=\scriptsize] at (0,0.85) {extremity}; \draw (S2)--(P2); \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (0,-1.15) {allowed}; \end{scope} \begin{scope}[shift={(6.6,0)}, local bounding box=no1] \node[box] (P3a) at (-0.65,0.35) {$C_m$}; \node[box] (P3b) at (0.65,0.35) {$C_m$}; \draw[dashed] (P3a)--(P3b); \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (0,-1.15) {forbidden}; \end{scope} \begin{scope}[shift={(9.9,0)}, local bounding box=no2] \node[box] (S4a) at (-0.7,0.35) {star}; \node[box] (S4b) at (0.7,0.35) {star}; \node[font=\scriptsize] at (0,0.85) {extremities}; \draw[dashed] (S4a)--(S4b); \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (0,-1.15) {forbidden}; \end{scope} \end{tikzpicture} \caption{Adjacencies in the reduced split-decomposition tree of a strict \(m\)-gonal cactus. The kernel \(\KC\) is the undirected sequence \(\mathrm{USEQ}_{m-1}(s_m)\)~\cite[Ch.~4]{BergeronLabelleLeroux1998}: \begin{equation} \label{eq:KC} \begin{aligned} m\text{ odd:}&\qquad \KC(x)=\tfrac12\bigl(s_m(x)^{m-1}+s_m(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}\bigr),\\ m\text{ even:}&\qquad \KC(x)=\tfrac12\bigl(s_m(x)^{m-1}+s_m(x)\,s_m(x^2)^{(m-2)/2}\bigr). \end{aligned} \end{equation} The unrooted series is given by the dissymmetry theorem \[ G_m(x)=T_{C_m}(x)+T_S(x)-T_{S\text{--}C_m}(x), \] with \(T_S=x\cdot\SC\), \(T_{S\text{--}C_m}=\KC\cdot\SX\), \(\SC=\exp(\sum\KC(x^i)/i)-1-\KC\), and \(T_{C_m}\) the dihedral cycle index \(Z_{D_m}\) evaluated at \(p_i=s_m(x^i)\). In particular \(G_m\) is a polynomial combination of the series \(s_m(x^i)\), hence has the same radius of convergence as \(s_m\). For \(|x|<\rho_m\) every \(s(x^i)\) with \(i\ge 2\) is analytic. The pair \((\rho_m,\tau_m)\) is the unique small positive solution of \begin{equation} \label{eq:crit} \Phi_m(\rho,\tau)=\tau,\qquad \partial_y\Phi_m(\rho,\tau)=1. \end{equation} Nonnegative coefficients imply that \(s(x)/x\) is increasing on \([0,\rho_m]\). \begin{theorem} \label{thm:tau-odd} If \(m\ge 5\) is odd, then \(\tau_m=\bigl(2/(m-1)\bigr)^{1/(m-1)}\). \end{theorem} \begin{proof} For \(m\) odd, \(\KC(x,y)=\frac12 y^{m-1}+g(x)\). Then \(\partial_y\Phi_m=x\,e^{\KC+h}\cdot\frac{m-1}{2}y^{m-2}\). At the critical point \(x\,e^{\KC+h}=\tau\), hence \(\frac{m-1}{2}\tau^{m-1}=1\). \end{proof} \begin{proposition} \label{prop:obstruction} If \(m\) is even, the same substitution yields \[ A(\rho)\,\tau+\tfrac{m-1}{2}\tau^{m-1}=1,\qquad A(\rho)=\tfrac12 s(\rho^2)^{(m-2)/2}, \] and \(A(\rho)\) is not a function of the pair \((\rho,\tau)\) alone. \end{proposition} \begin{proof} For \(m\) even, \(\KC(x,y)=\frac12 y^{m-1}+A(x)y\), so \(\partial_y\KC=\frac{m-1}{2}y^{m-2}+A(x)\). Substituting \(x\,e^{\KC+h}=\tau\) produces the displayed relation. The value \(s(\rho^2)\) is not determined by~\eqref{eq:crit}. \end{proof} Thus a one-term closed form for \(\tau_m\) is available if and only if the reflection count in~\eqref{eq:KC} produces no linear term in \(y\), i.e.\ if and only if \(m\) is odd. This is an obstruction to that substitution, not a proof that no expression of another kind exists. The series \(s_m(x)=x\,h(x^{m-1})\) reduces the arithmetic support to an aperiodic series \(h(y)\): indeed \(h(y)=1+y+O(y^2)\) for every \(m\ge 5\). The exponential schema~\cite[§VIII.4]{FlajoletSedgewick2009} applies to \(h\). \begin{theorem} \label{thm:asymp} As \(n\to\infty\) along \(n\equiv 1\pmod{m-1}\), \[ s_{m,n}\sim C_m\,\rho_m^{-n}\,n^{-3/2}. \] If \(m\) is odd, \(\Phi_{hh}=(m-1)\rho_m/\tau_m\) at the critical point of \(h\), and \(C_m\) is explicit in \(\rho_m,\tau_m\) up to the single numerical factor \(\Phi_y\). If \(m\) is even, every factor in \(C_m\) is numerical. \end{theorem} \begin{proof} Since \(s_m\) is supported only on \(n\equiv 1\pmod{m-1}\), it has \(m-1\) singularities of equal modulus on \(|x|=\rho_m\), and the single-singularity transfer theorem does not apply to \(s_m\) directly. Write \(y=x^{m-1}\) and \(s_m(x)=x\,h(y)\); the identity \(s_m(x^2)=x^2h(x^{2(m-1)})=x^2h(y^2)\) eliminates every fractional power of \(y\) from \(\KC\), for any \(m\): writing \(\KC(x^i)=y^i\kappa(y^i)\) for a well-defined \(\kappa\), one has \[ h(y)=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i\ge 1}\frac{y^i\kappa(y^i)}{i}\Bigr), \] a power series in \(y\) alone. \emph{Aperiodicity.} At lowest order, \(h(y)=\exp(y\kappa(0)+O(y^2))\), and \(\kappa(0)=\tfrac12[h(0)^{m-1}+h(0)^{(m-1)/2}]=1\) for every \(m\), since \(h(0)=1\). Hence \(h(y)=1+y+O(y^2)\): the coefficients of \(y^0\) and \(y^1\) are both exactly \(1\), for every \(m\ge5\). Two consecutive non-zero indices preclude any periodicity of the support (a period \(d>1\) would force every gap to be a multiple of \(d\), impossible with a gap of \(1\)). This holds structurally, independently of \(m\). \emph{Transfer.} The exp-schema transfer theorem~\cite[§VIII.4]{FlajoletSedgewick2009} therefore applies to \(h\) directly: writing \(y_c=\rho_m^{m-1}\) for its radius of convergence and \(h_c=h(y_c)=\tau_m/\rho_m\) for the critical value, one has \(h_k\sim(\gamma_m/(2\sqrt\pi))\,y_c^{-k}k^{-3/2}\), with \(\gamma_m=\sqrt{2y_c\Phi_y/\Phi_{hh}}\) evaluated at \((y_c,h_c)\) for \(\Phi(y,h)=\exp(G(y,h))\), \(G(y,h)=\sum_i y^i\kappa(y^i)/i\). \emph{The case \(m\) odd.} In the implicit-function schema, the \(h\)-dependence of \(G(y,h)\) resides entirely in its \(i=1\) term: the terms \(i\ge2\) involve \(h(y^i)\) at strictly smaller arguments and are analytic known functions of \(y\) alone at the critical point. From~\eqref{eq:KC} (\(m\) odd), that \(i=1\) term is \(y\cdot\tfrac12h^{m-1}\) plus \(h\)-independent tail contributions, so \(G_h=\tfrac12(m-1)y\,h^{m-2}\) and \(G_{hh}=\tfrac12(m-1)(m-2)y\,h^{m-3}=\bigl((m-2)/h\bigr)G_h\), identically. The criticality condition \(\Phi_h=1\), with \(\Phi(y_c,h_c)=h_c\), reads \(h_c\,G_h(y_c,h_c)=1\), i.e.\ \(G_h(y_c,h_c)=1/h_c\). Hence \(G_{hh}(y_c,h_c)=(m-2)/h_c^2\), and since \(\Phi_{hh}=\Phi\cdot(G_h^2+G_{hh})\): \[ \Phi_{hh}(y_c,h_c)=1/h_c+h_c\,G_{hh}=(m-1)/h_c=(m-1)\rho_m/\tau_m, \] a closed form. By contrast \(\Phi_y(y_c,h_c)\) depends on the tail terms and has no known closed form; it is evaluated numerically for each \(m\). \emph{The case \(m\) even.} The reduction above and the aperiodicity argument used no parity assumption, so the same transfer applies to \(h\) verbatim. However \(G_{hh}\) no longer reduces to \((m-2)/h_c^2\) alone: substituting \(s_m(x)=x\,h(y)\) into the even branch of~\eqref{eq:KC} gives, for the \(i=1\) term, \[ G_1(y,h)=y\cdot\tfrac12\bigl[h^{m-1}+h\cdot h(y^2)^{(m-2)/2}\bigr], \] an extra term linear in \(h\), with coefficient \(B(y)=\tfrac12h(y^2)^{(m-2)/2}\) a known analytic function of \(y\) alone at the critical point. This extra term vanishes under a second derivative, so \(G_{hh}(y_c,h_c)=\tfrac12(m-1)(m-2)y_ch_c^{m-3}\) still holds as a formula in \(y_c,h_c\) alone -- but it does \emph{not} vanish in \(G_h\). The criticality relation \(h_c\,G_h(y_c,h_c)=1\) now reads \(h_c[\tfrac12(m-1)y_ch_c^{m-2}+y_cB(y_c)]=1\), so \[ \Phi_{hh}(y_c,h_c)=(m-1)/h_c-(m-2)\,y_c\,B(y_c), \] rather than the closed sub-formula of the odd case: the linear term is structurally absent for \(m\) odd (the sole exponent \(m-1\) is odd there), and does not disappear for \(m\) even. Both \(\Phi_y\) and this correction term are evaluated numerically. \emph{Conclusion.} Translating \(h_k\sim(\gamma_m/(2\sqrt\pi))y_c^{-k}k^{-3/2}\) back via \(n=1+(m-1)k\) gives \(C_m=(\gamma_m/(2\sqrt\pi))\rho_m(m-1)^{3/2}\), with \(\gamma_m=\sqrt{2y_c\Phi_y/\Phi_{hh}}\) computed as above -- in closed form up to the single numerical factor \(\Phi_y\) for \(m\) odd, and entirely numerical for \(m\) even. \end{proof} The unrooted coefficients obey the same exponential growth \(\rho_m^{-n}\), since \(G_m\) has radius \(\rho_m\). \section{Strict monotonicity of the radius} \label{sec:mono} \begin{lemma} \label{lem:criterion} The map \(x\mapsto\partial_y\Phi_m\bigl(x,s_m(x)\bigr)\) increases from \(0\) to \(1\) on \([0,\rho_m)\). Consequently \(\rho_m<\rho_{m+1}\) if and only if \(\partial_y\Phi_{m+1}\bigl(\rho_m,s_{m+1}(\rho_m)\bigr)<1\). \end{lemma} \begin{proof} \(\partial_y\KC(x,y)\) is, by both branches of~\eqref{eq:KC}, a polynomial in \(y\) with non-negative coefficients, so \(x\mapsto\partial_y\Phi_m(x,s_m(x))=x\,e^{\KC+h}\,\partial_y\KC(x,s_m(x))\) is a product of non-negative, non-decreasing factors on \([0,\rho_m)\) (each factor of \(s_m\), \(e^{\KC+h}\), and \(\partial_y\KC\) has non-negative Taylor coefficients, hence is non-decreasing on the positive axis), hence itself non-decreasing; it is \(0\) at \(x=0\) and equals \(1\) at \(x=\rho_m\) by the defining system~\eqref{eq:crit}. The equivalence follows by evaluating this monotone map for \(\Phi_{m+1}\) at \(x=\rho_m\) instead of \(x=\rho_{m+1}\). \end{proof} \begin{lemma} \label{lem:elem} Let \(f\) be strictly convex and strictly increasing on an interval containing \(u\le\tau\), with \(f(u)=u\) and \(f(\tau)<\tau\). Then \(f'(u)<1\). \end{lemma} \begin{proof} If \(f'(u)\ge 1\), then \(f'>1\) on \((u,\tau]\), so \(f(\tau)-f(u)>\tau-u\), hence \(f(\tau)>\tau\). \end{proof} \begin{lemma} \label{lem:tau-lt-1} For every \(m\ge 5\), \(\tau_m\le\bigl(2/(m-1)\bigr)^{1/(m-1)}<1\). \end{lemma} \begin{proof} Equality holds for \(m\) odd. For \(m\) even, \(A(\rho)\tau+\frac{m-1}{2}\tau^{m-1}=1\) with \(A\ge 0\). \end{proof} Let \(\Phi_m\) denote the operator~\eqref{eq:Phi} at parameter \(m\), and define Picard iterates by \(s_m^{(0)}(x)=x\) and \(s_m^{(k+1)}=\Phi_m(x,s_m^{(k)})\). Nonnegative coefficients and the implicit species theorem~\cite[Prop.~2.5]{Pivoteau2012} give \(s_m^{(k)}\uparrow s_m\) on \([0,\rho_m]\). Each iterate has nonnegative coefficients, hence is increasing as a function of \(x\). \begin{theorem} \label{thm:weak} For every \(m\ge 5\), every \(k\ge 0\) and every \(x\in[0,\rho_m]\), \[ s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x)\le s_m^{(k)}(x). \] In particular \(s_{m+1}(\rho_m)\le\tau_m\), so \(s_{m+1}(\rho_m)\) is finite. \end{theorem} \begin{proof} The case \(k=0\) is an equality. Assume the claim at rank \(k\), for every index \(\ge 5\) and every point of the corresponding interval. Fix \(m\ge 5\) and \(x\in[0,\rho_m]\). Write \(u=s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x)\) and \(v=s_m^{(k)}(x)\), so \(0\le u\le v\). The same induction at the point \(x^2\le x\le\rho_m\) gives \(s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)\le s_m^{(k)}(x^2)\). Moreover \(v\le s_m(x)\le s_m(\rho_m)=\tau_m<1\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:tau-lt-1}, hence \(u<1\). \emph{Case \(m\) odd} (so \(m+1\) is even). Then \begin{align*} \KC_m(x)&=\tfrac12\bigl(v^{m-1}+s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}\bigr),\\ \KC_{m+1}(x)&=\tfrac12\bigl(u^{m}+u\cdot s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}\bigr). \end{align*} Since \(0\le u\le v<1\), \[ u^{m}=u\cdot u^{m-1}\le u^{m-1}\le v^{m-1}. \] For the reflection term, \[ u\cdot s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}\le s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}\le s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-1)/2}, \] using \(u\le 1\) and the induction at \(x^2\). Thus \(\KC_{m+1}(x)\le\KC_m(x)\). The same comparison at every \(x^i\), \(i\ge 2\), yields \(h_{m+1}(x)\le h_m(x)\), hence \(\Phi_{m+1}(x,u)\le\Phi_m(x,v)\), which is the claim at rank \(k+1\). \emph{Case \(m\) even} (so \(m+1\) is odd). Then \begin{align*} \KC_m(x)&=\tfrac12\bigl(v^{m-1}+v\cdot s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-2)/2}\bigr),\\ \KC_{m+1}(x)&=\tfrac12\bigl(u^{m}+s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{m/2}\bigr). \end{align*} Again \(u^{m}\le v^{m-1}\). For the reflection, \[ s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{m/2} =s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)\cdot s_{m+1}^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-2)/2} \le s_m^{(k)}(x^2)\cdot s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-2)/2}. \] Since \(s_m^{(k)}\) is increasing and \(x^2\le x\), one has \(s_m^{(k)}(x^2)\le s_m^{(k)}(x)=v\), hence \[ s_m^{(k)}(x^2)\cdot s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-2)/2} \le v\cdot s_m^{(k)}(x^2)^{(m-2)/2}. \] Thus \(\KC_{m+1}(x)\le\KC_m(x)\), and the argument closes as before. Passing to \(k\to\infty\) gives \(s_{m+1}(x)\le s_m(x)\) on \([0,\rho_m]\). \end{proof} The two parities cannot be merged: the even-to-odd step uses monotonicity of the iterates in the argument \(x^20\)). Both monomials of \(\widetilde{\KC}_{m+1}(\rho_m,\tau_m)\) are therefore strictly smaller than the corresponding monomials of \(\KC_m(\rho_m)\), so \(\widetilde{\KC}_{m+1}(\rho_m,\tau_m)<\KC_m(\rho_m)\) already at this leading term. \emph{Case \(m\) even} (so \(m+1\) is odd). By~\eqref{eq:KC}, \begin{align*} \widetilde{\KC}_{m+1}(\rho_m,\tau_m)&=\tfrac12\bigl(\tau_m^{m}+s_m(\rho_m^2)^{m/2}\bigr),\\ \KC_m(\rho_m)&=\tfrac12\bigl(\tau_m^{m-1}+\tau_m\,s_m(\rho_m^2)^{(m-2)/2}\bigr). \end{align*} As above \(\tau_m^{m}<\tau_m^{m-1}\). For the second monomial, \(s_m\) is increasing and \(\rho_m^2<\rho_m\), so \(s_m(\rho_m^2)\le s_m(\rho_m)=\tau_m\); hence \[ s_m(\rho_m^2)^{m/2}=s_m(\rho_m^2)\cdot s_m(\rho_m^2)^{(m-2)/2}\le\tau_m\cdot s_m(\rho_m^2)^{(m-2)/2}, \] matching the second monomial of \(\KC_m(\rho_m)\) exactly. Again \(\widetilde{\KC}_{m+1}(\rho_m,\tau_m)<\KC_m(\rho_m)\), strictly by the first monomial alone. In both cases the leading term already gives a strict inequality; the termwise tail comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:weak}, applied at \(x=\rho_m^i\), \(i\ge2\), with \(s_m(\rho_m^i)<1\) in place of \((\rho_m,\tau_m)\), gives \(\tilde h_{m+1}(\rho_m)\le h_m(\rho_m)\) for the remaining terms, non-strictly. The exponent of \(\widetilde\Phi_{m+1}(\rho_m,\tau_m)\) is therefore strictly smaller than that of \(\Phi_m(\rho_m,\tau_m)=\tau_m/\rho_m\), and so is the exponential after multiplication by \(\rho_m\). \end{proof} \begin{lemma} \label{lem:convex} For \(x\in(0,\rho_m]\), the map \(y\mapsto\Phi_m(x,y)\) is strictly convex on \((0,\infty)\). \end{lemma} \begin{proof} Both branches of~\eqref{eq:KC} satisfy \(\partial_y^2\KC>0\) for \(y>0\) and \(m\ge 5\), and \(x\,e^{\KC+h}>0\). \end{proof} \begin{theorem} \label{thm:mono} For every \(m\ge 5\), \(\rho_m<\rho_{m+1}\). Equivalently, \(\mu_m=1/\rho_m\) is strictly decreasing. \end{theorem} \begin{proof} Let \(f(y)=\Phi_{m+1}(\rho_m,y)\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}, \(f\) is strictly convex; it is also strictly increasing, \(u:=s_{m+1}(\rho_m)\) is a fixed point of \(f\), and \(u\le\tau_m\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:weak}. Lemma~\ref{lem:aux} gives \(f(\tau_m)<\tau_m\). Lemma~\ref{lem:elem} yields \(f'(u)<1\). Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} yields \(\rho_m<\rho_{m+1}\). \end{proof} \section{\texorpdfstring{The large-\(m\) limit}{The large-m limit}} \label{sec:limit} Throughout this section write \(n=m-1\) and \(u_m=n\bigl(\KC(\rho_m)+h_m(\rho_m)\bigr)\), so that \(\tau_m=\rho_m e^{u_m/n}\) and \(\rho_m\le\tau_m\). \begin{lemma} \label{lem:tails} \(\KC(\rho_m)+h_m(\rho_m)\to 0\). More precisely \(h_m(\rho_m)=O(m^{-2})\) and \(\KC(\rho_m)=O(m^{-1})\). \end{lemma} \begin{proof} By Lemma~\ref{lem:tau-lt-1}, \(\tau_m^{m-1}\le 2/n\). Since \(s_m(x)/x\) is increasing, \(s_m(\rho_m^i)\le\tau_m\rho_m^{i-1}\). Hence \[ s_m(\rho_m^i)^{m-1}\le\frac2n\,\rho_m^{n(i-1)}, \] and \(\rho_m^{n}\le\tau_m^{n}\le 2/n\). Summing the identity part of the tail gives \[ \sum_{i\ge 2}\frac1i\,s_m(\rho_m^i)^{m-1}\le\frac2n\cdot\frac{\rho_m^{n}}{1-\rho_m^{n}}\le\frac4{n(n-2)}. \] The reflection part of \(h_m\) is of the same order. The bound on \(\KC(\rho_m)\) is \(\frac1n+\frac12\tau_m^{n/2}=O(m^{-1})\). \end{proof} \begin{theorem} \label{thm:rho-to-1} One has \(\rho_m\to 1\) and \(\mu_m\to 1\). Combined with Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}, the sequence \((\rho_m)_{m\ge 5}\) is strictly increasing and convergent to \(1\). \end{theorem} \begin{proof} Lemmas~\ref{lem:tau-lt-1} and~\ref{lem:tails} give \(\tau_m\to 1\) and \(u_m/n\to 0\), hence \(\rho_m=\tau_m e^{-u_m/n}\to 1\). \end{proof} Let \(\Omega=W(e^{-1})\) be the principal Lambert value~\cite{Corless1996} satisfying \(\Omega e^{\Omega}=e^{-1}\), so \(\Omega\approx 0.27846454276\) and \(1+\Omega\) is the unique root of \(u=1+e^{-u}\) in \([1,2]\). Set \begin{equation} \label{eq:lambda} \lambda_m=\frac{\log(n/2)+1+\Omega}{n}. \end{equation} \begin{theorem} \label{thm:equiv} As \(m\to\infty\), \begin{equation} \label{eq:equiv} 1-\rho_m=\lambda_m-\tfrac12\lambda_m^2+O\Bigl(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\Bigr). \end{equation} In particular \(1-\rho_m\sim(\log m)/m\) and \(\mu_m=1+\lambda_m+O\bigl((\log m)^2/m^2\bigr)\). Moreover \(\tau_m^{m-1}\sim 2/(m-1)\) in both parities, and \(u_m=1+\Omega+O(m^{-1})\). \end{theorem} \begin{proof} We first take \(m\) odd, so \(\tau_m^{n}=2/n\) exactly. \emph{Reduction of \(s_m(\rho_m^2)\).} Write \(\delta_m=s_m(\rho_m^2)/\rho_m^2-1\). The first nonlinear term of \(s_m\) has degree \(m\), and \(s_m(\rho_m)-\rho_m=O(m^{-1})\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}. For degrees \(\ge m\), \(\rho_m^{2j}\le\rho_m^m\rho_m^{j}\), hence \(\delta_m=O(\rho_m^{m-2})=O(m^{-2})\). In fact the leading contribution is \(\delta_m\sim\rho_m^{2n}=O(m^{-2})\). \emph{Fixed point for \(u_m\).} Then \(s_m(\rho_m^2)^{n/2}=\rho_m^{n}(1+\delta_m)^{n/2}=\rho_m^{n}(1+O(m^{-1}))\), and \(\rho_m^{n}=(2/n)e^{-u_m}\). Thus \[ n\,\KC(\rho_m)=1+e^{-u_m}(1+O(m^{-1})). \] The tail satisfies \(n\,h_m(\rho_m)=O(m^{-1})\): the summand \(i=2\) is \(O(\rho_m^{2n}n)=O(m^{-1})\), and \(i\ge 3\) is smaller. Therefore \(u_m=1+e^{-u_m}+O(m^{-1})\). Linearising \(u\mapsto u-1-e^{-u}\) at \(1+\Omega\), where the derivative is \(1+\Omega\neq 0\), yields \(u_m=1+\Omega+O(m^{-1})\). \emph{Transfer.} Now \(-\log\rho_m=\bigl(\log(n/2)+u_m\bigr)/n=\lambda_m+O(m^{-2})\). The expansion \(1-e^{-\Lambda}=\Lambda-\Lambda^2/2+O(\Lambda^3)\) with \(\Lambda=-\log\rho_m\) gives~\eqref{eq:equiv}. \emph{Even \(m\).} Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} reads \(A(\rho_m)\tau_m+\frac n2\tau_m^{n}=1\). As above \(s_m(\rho_m^2)\sim\rho_m^2\), so \[ A(\rho_m)\tau_m=\tfrac12 s_m(\rho_m^2)^{(n-1)/2}\tau_m\sim\tfrac12\rho_m^{n-1}\tau_m\sim\tfrac12\rho_m^{n}. \] Combined with the critical relation this forces \(\tau_m^{n}\sim 2/n\), exactly as in the odd case. We now determine the precise order of \(A(\rho_m)\tau_m\) rather than only its crude majorant. Using \(\tau_m^{n-1}\sim\tau_m^n/\tau_m\sim2/n\) (since \(\tau_m\to1\)) and \(\tau_m\to1\) again, \[ A(\rho_m)\tau_m\sim\tfrac12\rho_m^{n-1}\tau_m\sim\tfrac1n\rho_m^{n}\cdot\tau_m/\rho_m\sim\tfrac1n\rho_m^n, \] and since \(\rho_m^n=\tau_m^ne^{-u_m}\sim(2/n)e^{-u_m}\), this gives the precise asymptotic \(A(\rho_m)\tau_m\sim e^{-u_m}/n\) -- not merely \(O(m^{-1})\), but a leading coefficient tied self-consistently to \(u_m\) itself. Consequently \[ n\,\KC(\rho_m)=n\bigl(\tfrac12\tau_m^{n}+A\tau_m\bigr)=1+(n-1)A(\rho_m)\tau_m\sim1+\frac{n-1}{n}e^{-u_m}\longrightarrow1+e^{-u_m} \] as \(n\to\infty\), recovering exactly the same self-consistent relation \(n\,\KC(\rho_m)\sim1+e^{-u_m}\) as the odd case, term by term rather than merely up to an unspecified \(O(m^{-1})\). The tail estimate \(n\,h_m(\rho_m)=O(m^{-1})\) is established by the same argument as the odd case, without using parity, so \(u_m=1+e^{-u_m}+O(m^{-1})\) follows exactly as before, and the same linearisation at \(1+\Omega\) applies. The transfer step did not use parity. \end{proof} The second term \(-\frac12\lambda_m^2\) is forced by \(1-e^{-\lambda}=\lambda-\lambda^2/2\); it is not an adjustable correction. \begin{remark}[Why a Lambert-\(W\) constant appears] The appearance of \(\Omega=W(e^{-1})\) is not particular to cactus graphs. It is a generic feature of the smooth implicit-function schema~\cite[§VII.4]{FlajoletSedgewick2009} whenever the dominant term of the kernel is a single power \(y^{d}\) homogeneous of some degree \(d\) growing with the parameter (here \(d=m-1\)): writing the criticality condition \(\Phi_h=1\) forces, after the scaling \(u=d(\KC(\rho)+h(\rho))\) used throughout this section, a fixed-point equation of the shape \(u=c+e^{-u}\) for some constant \(c\) depending only on the schema's normalisation (here \(c=1\), from the single homogeneous term \(\tfrac12h^{m-1}\)), whose solution is \(u=c+W(e^{-c})\) by the same substitution \(v=u-c\), \(v\,e^{v}=e^{-c}\) used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv}. The classical Cayley-tree equation \(T=xe^{T}\) is the best-known instance producing a Lambert-\(W\) singularity by this same general mechanism; the present equation \(u=1+e^{-u}\) is a different instance of it, arising from a different schema, not the Cayley equation evaluated at a matching point. What is shared is the mechanism -- a single dominant homogeneous term meeting the criticality condition -- not the equation itself. \end{remark} \section{Numerical values} \label{sec:num} Table~\ref{tab:crit} records solutions of~\eqref{eq:crit}. The identity \(\Phi_{hh}=(m-1)\rho_m/\tau_m\) for odd \(m\), and the constant \(1+W(e^{-1})\) in \(\lambda_m\), both come from the same structural fact: the outer kernel is a single homogeneous term of degree \(m-1\), up to a reflection term within \(\KC\) itself (not the outer tail \(h_m\) of Section~\ref{sec:sing}) that scales to \(e^{-u}\) after multiplication by \(m-1\). A one-term closed form for \(\tau_m\) when \(m\) is even is not supplied by the critical substitution, and the infinite orbit \(\rho,\rho^2,\rho^4,\dots\) makes a finite algebraic elimination of \(s_m(\rho_m^2)\) implausible. The equivalent \(\tau_m^{m-1}\sim 2/(m-1)\) remains valid. No species injection explaining \(\rho_m<\rho_{m+1}\) is known. Extensions to a mixed set \(\Omega\), or to block-cacti, change the kernel and remove the successor \(m\mapsto m+1\); they are not treated here. \section*{Data availability} Scripts verifying the enumerative coefficients of Tables~\ref{tab:rooted}--\ref{tab:unrooted} (Sections~\ref{sec:grammar}--\ref{sec:sing}) are available at \url{https://github.com/frederic-speyser/non-plane-mgonal-cacti} and archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21461100}.
